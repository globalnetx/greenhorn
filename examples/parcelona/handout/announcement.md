# Email to everyone at Parcelona

**Subject:** Meet our new colleague

Hi all,

We have a new colleague, and it is not a person. Over the last few
weeks Lena and I have been teaching our AI assistant what Parcelona
does and does not do, how we write to customers, and the rules we
agreed for using AI. Your judgment stays yours; it takes the dull parts
off your plate. We gave it a name, Estiba, and I asked it to introduce
itself; the next paragraph is its own words.

In its own words: "I am Estiba, Parcelona's AI assistant, not a person,
and I will say so whenever anyone asks. Ana and Lena taught me who
Parcelona is, and I have read the website, the customer guide, and a
stack of the emails and quotes you actually sent. I am here for the writing that
eats your afternoons: the first draft of a reply, a delay notice, a
booking confirmation, the explanation of an invoice line. I draft; you
read it, change what you like, and send it under your name. Brief me
the way you would brief a colleague who started this week: which
shipment, what happened, who the reader is, what a good answer looks
like. And when you are not sure how to ask, ask me: how should I brief
you for this? And I check with you before anything is created,
changed, booked, sent, published, or costs money."

Try it this week on something real. Coordinators: paste a customer's
email and say "ask me what you need to know, then draft the reply".
Operations: hand it a carrier notice and ask for "an internal update
for the Barcelona team: our reference, what changed, what we do next,
five lines". Sales: "a first-quote email to Nordlicht Optics for Ningbo
to Rotterdam, from quote QT-204511, warm and short, nothing about rates
beyond what the quote says".

Four ground rules, agreed with the managing director. Staff records and
payroll, private individuals' personal data, carrier buy rates and
margins, and anything a customer marked confidential never go into an
AI. Our systems only through the routes we provide: the connector, or
Cargo Desk's screens with you driving, never a script with your login.
A person decides before anything is created, changed, booked, sent,
published, or costs money; a draft you send yourself is yours to check. And it never
pretends to be a person; a draft goes out under your name, and we are
open with customers that we use AI for writing. The handout attached
has the rest, in ten minutes.

Questions, ideas, wins, and things it got wrong all go to #ask-the-ai,
or to me. "It got this wrong" is welcome news: every wrong answer that
reaches me becomes a fix for everyone.

Ana, customer coordinator, Barcelona
