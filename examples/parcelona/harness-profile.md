## Vocabulary

- Reference formats: QT- plus six digits for quotes, BK- plus six for
  bookings, SH- plus the booking digits and a two-digit sequence for
  shipments, CL- year and running number for claims, PT- plus five for
  tickets, PI- and PC- with year and running number for invoices and
  credit notes. [you said]
- A quote is not a booking, and nothing moves until a booking exists.
  One booking can cover several containers or pieces; status is per
  shipment. A claim is not an admission of liability. Tickets are
  customer questions logged in Cargo Desk, and not every email becomes
  one. A number that does not fit its pattern is a typo or something
  else: ask, never correct it silently. [you said]
- Contracts usually name Incoterms 2020. The Incoterm decides who pays
  us: a quote for an FOB shipment goes to the buyer, not the factory.
  [you said]
- Customers mix up demurrage and detention, and call all of it
  demurrage. As we use them: demurrage runs while the full container
  sits in the terminal past its free days; detention while it is out at
  the customer's door past its free days; some lines bill both as one
  "D&D" charge; terminal storage is a third charge, from the terminal.
  Each has its own free days. [you said]
- The carrier issues the master bill (MBL) to us and we issue the house
  bill (HBL) to the customer; customers ask about the HBL, the carrier
  only knows the MBL. The same pair in air freight: HAWB from us, MAWB
  from the airline. [you said]
- Chargeable weight in air freight is the higher of actual and volume
  weight; light, bulky cargo billed on volume surprises customers, and
  the quote states which applied. [you said]
- Missing a cut-off means the next sailing or flight. LCL takes longer
  than FCL at both ends. ETD and ETA are the carrier's estimates. [you
  said]
- Colleagues say "DUA" and "levante" in Spanish. "The DUA is released"
  means customs has cleared the goods; "tenemos levante" means customs
  is done, and the carrier and the terminal still have to release the
  container. [you said]

## Values and voice

- Mined from eleven sent pieces (seven customer emails, two quotes, two
  internal updates): replies open with the answer, run six to ten lines,
  say no in one sentence with the alternative in the next, never
  apologise twice, never close with an offer of further help. The two
  wince-at pieces were long, hedged, and ended with a summary. [documents:
  sent writing sample, 2026-09]
- Register chosen by example: "warm and direct" for everyday replies
  (over "friendly but formal" and "brisk"); "calm and factual" for delay
  and bad-news notes. The chosen and rejected samples are in the voice
  skill's examples, recast with Tulipware. [you said]
- Everyday sample tuned: a reply to Tulipware about delivering ten
  pallets of a shipment early. "Friendly but formal" rejected as too
  stiff for a customer we write to every week; "brisk" rejected as
  curt. Sober sample tuned: the Tulipware delay notice. [you said]
- The quiet rule confirmed as the default: say what the reader needs and
  no more; nothing spelled out that they can infer. [you said]

- Sober for delays, damage claims, invoice disputes, customs holds. [you said]
