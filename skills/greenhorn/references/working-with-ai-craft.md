# The craft of working with an AI colleague

The general part: what holds for any company. The working-with-ai skill
carries it in short, with the company's own examples. The handout's
fixed text rests on it. And it is what to say when the admin asks why a
line is there, or wants the working-with-ai skill to say more. It is
background for the assistant, never read out and never copied whole
into a skill.

## The five-part brief

Most people meet the assistant as a chatbot and type into it the way
they type into a search box: "give me x". That leaves it to guess, and
a guess is where wrong answers come from. The five-part brief (task,
context, reader, what good looks like, why) is how anyone would brief
a sharp new colleague, and it works for the same reason.

## The three phases

Curious: a smart assistant that answers and drafts. Enabled: a thinking
partner on analysis and decisions. Native: it handles repetitive work
in the background, with people owning the judgment. The phases set
expectations, so nobody expects background automation on day one and
nobody dismisses it as a search box either. Moving on is a choice the
company makes, recorded in the profile.

## The habits

The habits are the ones that separate people who get value from the
assistant from people who try it twice: ask it how to ask, let it ask
first, treat it as a conversation, hand over the document, ask for the
draft and edit, one job per conversation, role-play the reader, check
what costs money or trust, keep excluded data out, and say how much
you want, since the assistant is told to say what the reader needs
and no more. A skill carries the ones that matter at this company; a
handout names three or four, never all ten.

## Ask it how to ask

The first habit is the one people forget: the assistant itself is the
best person to ask how to work with it. "How should I brief you for
this?", "what do you need from me?", "is this too vague?", "how could
I have asked that better?" It knows what it needs and says so, and it
gets people good at this faster than any handout. That is why the
line sits in the habits, and why the working-with-ai skill carries it
in its "How to ask" list.

## The company's own examples

The examples are the company's own because a handout full of generic
advice is skimmed and forgotten, and one with the reader's own Tuesday
in it is kept. And the story is the admin's because they were there.
